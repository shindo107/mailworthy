Mailworthy clears your bags at the mailbox. One click sends everything worth more on the auction house (or worth more disenchanted) to your bank alt, sends your friends and guildmates the crafting materials they need, and leaves the rest in your bags for the vendor. No more checking prices item by item, no more dragging twelve items into a mail at a time.

Made for World of Warcraft: Forever. Works with mouse and keyboard, and with a controller.

How it works

  1. Open any mailbox and click the Send Mail tab: the Mailworthy window opens beside it. (The Mailworthy button under the mail window opens and closes it too.)
  2. Check the Mailworthy window: who gets mail, what will be mailed and why, and what stays in your bags and why. Click any item to keep it.
  3. Click Send. Mailworthy fills as many mails as it needs (12 stacks each), for every recipient, and sends them all with that one click.
  4. Sell what's left to the nearest vendor: after sending, the items worth more at a vendor (grey junk too) carry a small V tag in your bags. Changed your mind about one? Lock it and the tag goes.

Pick your bank alt with Choose... in the settings, from the characters you've logged into on this realm and faction, so there are no typos. Bank alt on a second WoW account? Type its name, or press This character is my bank alt on the alt itself.

No bank alt? No problem. The window still opens and mails your friends' materials. What's worth selling stays in your bags with the reason, and your bags carry the same AH / B / DE / V tags a bank alt would see. Skip once beside your bank alt keeps its share in your bags for one visit.

What gets mailed

For every item in your bags (all its stacks together, in every bag you carry, reagent bag included), Mailworthy compares what you'd get from a vendor with what it's worth on the auction house after the 5% auction cut, the listing deposit and the postage (30 copper per stack). An item is mailed when it's clearly worth it: by default at least 1 silver and 25% more than the vendor pays. Both numbers are adjustable.

No math needed on your side. The price is your last Auctionator scan, or the 3-day median of your scans when that's lower, so one empty-market spike can't make a dud look valuable. Half the deposit counts, since the game refunds it on a sale; a flooded market is valued at its floor; and the rule knows your realm's launch day. Before the first raids, enchanting materials, herbs and elementals get a B verdict: bank them on your alt, they sell for more later. As the realm ages and gold piles up, the minimum gain grows with it.

No auction price yet? On a fresh realm plenty of things haven't been listed yet. Instead of sitting in your main's bags, an item with no auction price (or only one more than 7 days old) goes to your bank alt to wait for a price, and the window says so. Common trade goods don't wait for a price: with none (or an old one) they're mailed anyway, and with a price they follow the usual rule. They are cloth, leather and hides, ore, bars and stone, herbs, elementals, and enchanting dust, essences and shards. Grey junk stays for the vendor.

Mailworthy never mails:

  • items you've locked;
  • soulbound and quest items, and anything an active quest asks for (Crisp Spider Meat for Redridge Goulash stays, and its tooltip names the quest);
  • the materials your own professions still use (see below);
  • your profession tools (fishing poles, Mining Pick, Blacksmith Hammer, Skinning Knife and the like);
  • gold.

Your professions come first

Mailworthy knows which professions the character has and keeps their materials in the bags, whatever they'd sell for: cloth for a tailor (or for first aid), leather for a leatherworker, metal and stone for a blacksmith or an engineer (plus parts, explosives and devices), herbs for an alchemist, dust, essences and shards for an enchanter, meat and fish for a cook. The window shows them under "staying" with the reason, for example your First Aid needs it (until skill 150). Elemental materials are still mailed; lock them if you want to keep them. The whole rule is one checkbox in the settings, on by default.

  • Levelled past it? It goes. Once every recipe that uses a material is grey for you, it's mailed to be sold: at First Aid 80 your linen goes, your wool stays. The thresholds come from a recipe-by-recipe read of every Forever profession, and when you open your own profession window Mailworthy checks them against your recipe colours.
  • Sell what I gather (off by default): a miner who is also a blacksmith, or an herbalist who is also an alchemist, can mail what they gather to the auction house instead of keeping it.

Send a profession's materials to a friend

Got a guildmate who enchants, or a friend levelling leatherworking? Bind a profession to them once and Mailworthy mails them every material it uses, every time you press Send, until you remove the binding. Type /mw friends (or press Friends... in the settings), pick the player from your party, your guild or your own characters (online players first, with their professions shown and suggested), then pick the profession. Two clicks, or two presses on the controller.

  • The Mailworthy window lists your bindings above the mail list, each with Skip once (just this visit) and Remove. Every recipient gets a line under To:, and items headed to a friend show to Bob instead of a gain.
  • An Enchanting binding also takes your green and blue weapons and armor, for them to disenchant. Its Gear button narrows that to pieces that disenchant for more than they would sell for, at auction or to a vendor; the rest follow your normal rules.
  • Recipes go to the friend bound to their profession: Plans: Iridescent Hammer to your blacksmith. With no binding for it, a recipe goes to your bank alt like anything else.
  • Class-only items (a scroll or piece of gear that says "Classes: Mage") can go to your mage friend: pick the player and "Mage items" is the first choice. It takes only what your own class can't use.
  • Each binding has a price cap, 10 silver each to start; the - and + buttons step it from 50 copper to 100 gold, or to no cap. Anything that sells for the cap or more each goes to your bank alt instead, and the scan tells you why.
  • Once your friend has levelled past a material, it stops going to them and goes to your bank alt. Mailworthy learns their skill from their own Mailworthy, the guild roster, or your own character's last login.
  • Bindings cover all your characters on that realm and faction, or just one; your call per binding.
  • Locked items still stay, and a binding beats "keep my professions' materials" for that profession, since you asked for it.
  • If your friend runs Mailworthy too, the addons tell each other which of their characters craft what, and your mail goes to whichever of their characters has the profession: the friend themself, or their crafter alt. Never their bank alt. The window always names who gets it. (/mw feature comms off stops the messages.)
  • Wherever Mailworthy names a player (Friends, the guild window, the To: lines, the ledger), the Mailworthy icon after their name means they run Mailworthy too.

One template for the whole guild

Guild masters and officers can set who gets each profession's materials for the whole guild: herbs to the alchemist, dust and greens to the enchanter, cloth to the tailor. Open /mw guild (or Guild... in the settings), press Add a rule... for each profession (each with its price cap) and press Publish to guild.

  • Guildmates running Mailworthy get it in chat and in their Guild window. Nothing is mailed until they press Use guild rules once; after that, updates apply on their own, each with a chat line.
  • One press turns it off: Guild rules: on, in the Guild window or on the mailbox window's Bindings heading.
  • Your own bindings and your own professions always come first: a tailor keeps their cloth. Skip once works on guild rules too.
  • Guild contributions: Mailworthy counts how many items each character mails because of the guild's rules (stack units: 20 Strange Dust is 20), per day for the last 35 days and all time. Your own bindings, your bank alt, Return to sender and mail to your own characters never count. The counts are shared with your guild, so a guild using Raid Ledger with LedgerLink can thank its biggest givers, even ones who never export themselves. Only the counts go, never what the items were or who got them.
  • Any guildmate running Mailworthy passes a copy on, so the guild master doesn't need to be online. A copy is taken only when your own guild roster shows its author as the guild master or an officer, and only its rules for guild members.

Optional: Auctionator or TradeSkillMaster, Disenchantry and Siz DisenchantValue

Mailworthy works on its own, and gets smarter with these addons installed:

  • Auctionator (recommended) provides the auction house prices from your own scans. With it, Mailworthy weighs every item's real value and only mails what's worth selling. When your prices are more than 3 days old, the Mailworthy button shows a !, and if a guildmate running Mailworthy has scanned recently, it tells you who.
  • TradeSkillMaster: without Auctionator, Mailworthy takes its prices from TSM instead (its AuctionDB minimum buyout, held down by its market value). Auctionator comes first when both are installed.
  • Disenchantry: with it installed, Mailworthy also compares the disenchant value of green, blue and purple gear (switched on by default), the same "Expected disenchant value" Disenchantry shows in the tooltip: the era disenchant tables priced with today's Auctionator prices. Gear that's worth more as enchanting materials goes to your bank alt to disenchant, and the scan says which source priced it.
  • Siz DisenchantValue: also switches the disenchant comparison on, and its numbers come first when both are installed, since it learns from your own disenchants.

Without Auctionator or TradeSkillMaster there are no prices to compare, so Mailworthy keeps it simple: it mails every tradeable white (common) item you haven't locked, plus the common trade goods above (dust, essences and shards too), and recipes go to the friend bound to their profession. Grey junk stays for the vendor.

On your bank alt

When you log in to your bank alt, every item Mailworthy sent shows what it's for, worked out again with today's auction prices on that character:

  • a small tag on the bag slot: AH to list on the auction house, B to bank for a later phase, DE to disenchant, or V if it's now better sold to a vendor;
  • a tooltip line with today's price and why it was mailed in the first place, so you can see when the market has moved;
  • a summary of what to list, what to disenchant and what to vendor: Summary... in the Mailworthy window, or /mw summary.

Your bank alt gets the Mailworthy window at the mailbox too. What it sells stays in its bags, while your bindings and Return to sender still mail from it.

Return to sender

Press Return to sender in the Mailworthy window, on any character, and everything another character mailed you joins the Mailing list to go back to them: whole stacks, up to what they sent and you haven't mailed back yet. Every sender gets their own To: line before you press Send. The button is lit while it's on, one more press brings the list back to normal, and it turns itself off when you close the mailbox. Locked and quest items never go back.

Mail ledger

Mailworthy keeps a log of every mail your characters send (its own and Blizzard's Send button) and everything you take out of the mailbox: date, who, subject, each item with its count and value. Open it with /mw ledger or Mail ledger... in the settings; Export log... copies all of it, so you can show where items went.

  • When another player has mailed you more than you've sent back, you see it: a chat line when you take the mail, a line at the top of the Mailworthy window, and the ledger. Pay back by mail, materials or gold, or press Mark paid if you settled another way.
  • Only your own side ever shows: nobody is listed as owing you. Your own characters, the auction house, system mail and COD mail don't count.

Tags in your bag addon and in Auctionator

The AH / B / DE / V tags and the blue lock glow show in Baganator, Bagnon, ArkInventory and BetterBags too, with the legend under their bag window, and on the item icons in Auctionator's Selling tab, so you can see what to post right there. Each one has its own off switch (/mw feature lists them).

Lock the items you want to keep

  • Mouse: Alt+click a bag item to lock or unlock it.
  • Controller: press L3 on an item in your bags.
  • Or click the item in the Mailworthy window (mouse or controller).
  • Locked items glow blue, like quest items, while the Mailworthy window is open at the mailbox, so your bags stay clean the rest of the time. Hover a blue item to see why it glows. When you lock an item elsewhere it glows for a moment to show it took. (A setting keeps the glow on everywhere.)
  • Locks stay wherever you move the item, even after sorting your bags. Mailworthy never mails a locked item.

Controller support

Mailworthy works with WoW Forever's controller interface. At a mailbox, switch to Send Mail (R1) and the Mailworthy window opens; Triangle (Y) closes and reopens it. In the Mailworthy window the D-pad moves between buttons, Cross presses and Circle closes it. The settings and Friends windows work the same way, wherever you open them.

Settings

  • Your bank alt (optional)
  • Minimum gain over the vendor price (silver and percent)
  • Auction listing length used for the deposit estimate
  • Include disenchant value
  • Keep my professions' materials (lists the character's crafting professions and their skill)
  • Sell what I gather
  • Bank alt tags on every character, or only on the bank alt
  • Locked-item glow: in the Mailworthy window only, or always
  • Friends (your bindings) and Guild (your guild's template)
  • Mail ledger, bank alt summary and the minimap button

Open them from the Mailworthy window, the minimap button, or type /mw.

Good to know

  • Mail only goes between characters on the same realm and faction, so that's who Mailworthy offers as your bank alt and as friends.
  • Mailworthy doesn't post auctions. It gets the items to your bank alt; you list them there (Auctionator makes that quick).
  • Nothing is sent without your click, nothing happens in combat, no gold is ever attached, and the window always shows who gets each item and why it's mailed or kept.
  • Mailworthys talk to each other over hidden addon messages to your guild and group: which characters craft what, their skill, the day of the last price scan, your guild's template, your Mailworthy version, and (to your guild only) how many items each character has mailed because of the guild's rules. The value of a mail goes in a whisper only between the two players who exchanged it, so both ledgers agree. Never your bags or gold.
  • Stay up to date: when someone in your guild or group runs a newer Mailworthy, one chat line a session (and a line in the Mailworthy and settings windows) tells you which version is out. /mw feature versionNotice off hides it.
  • Want the numbers? /mw scan shows the price, deposit, postage and gain behind every decision.
  • The full guide, page by page, is on the Mailworthy wiki.
